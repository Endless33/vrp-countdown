# VRP Countdown — Public Phase Closure

## Status

The public phase of VRP is coming to an end.

This decision was not made because the protocol failed.

It was made because my priorities have changed.

For many months I published architecture, demonstrations, validation reports, runtime evidence, repositories, documentation, and a public validation kit.

The objective was simple:

Allow people to observe.

Evaluate.

Question.

Challenge.

Test.

That objective has been fulfilled.

---

## Why This Is Changing

Building a protocol and continuously explaining it are two different jobs.

The first moves the project forward.

The second eventually begins slowing it down.

At some point every engineering project reaches a stage where protecting development becomes more valuable than explaining development.

For VRP, that moment has arrived.

---

## Public Validation Kit

The public Validation Kit will be removed from public availability.

Its purpose has been fulfilled.

Public demonstrations are no longer the highest priority.

The priority is research.

Protocol hardening.

Implementation.

Testing.

Architecture.

---

## Pilot Program

The public pilot request form will be closed.

The private pilot waiting list will also be paused.

Current pilot activity may be suspended while the protocol enters a new development phase.

This pause may last weeks or months.

No timeline is being promised.

No reopening date is being announced.

---

## Communication

Public updates will become significantly less frequent.

Direct messages are unlikely to remain an active communication channel.

Engineering time will be invested where it creates the most value:

inside the protocol.

Not inside endless discussions.

Not inside repeated explanations.

---

## Next Phase

The next phase is research.

Hardening.

Optimization.

Private experimentation.

Possibly entirely new projects built on the same architectural principles.

Progress will continue.

It simply won't happen in public.

---

## Final Note

Every opportunity has a window.

Public access was one of them.

The protocol continues to evolve.

The public phase does not.

Thank you to everyone who tested, questioned, challenged, and contributed with genuine engineering intent.

The countdown ends here.

The next chapter begins in silence.
